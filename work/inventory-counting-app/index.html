#007LOW-CODE APPSHIPPED

Inventory Counting App

A low-code app that guided shop-floor workers through the weekly stock count: what is on hand, how much, with which best-before date, and whether it can still be used.

ROLE
Design, build, roll out
YEAR
2024–2026
STACK
Google AppSheet · Google Sheets · data warehouse
STATUS
Shipped, used every week

01 · PROBLEM

At a global meal-kit company, every production week starts from one question: what stock do we actually have? Ingredients arrive in lots, each with its own best-before date, and what was left over from last week decides what can be used next.

Counting it by hand in spreadsheets was slow and hard to trust. Lots and best-before dates got lost, usable stock went to waste, and missing stock only showed up as a stockout in the middle of production.

02 · WHAT I BUILT

  • The data comes to the count. Every week a Google Sheet refreshes itself from the data warehouse: deliveries, last week's inventory, consumption, and every item's lot and best-before date.
  • A guided count on the shop floor. The app walks workers item by item through what should be on the shelf, so counting becomes checking: confirm the quantity, the lot and the best-before date.
  • Usable or not, at a glance. Each lot is checked against the coming production week, so stock that won't last is flagged before it becomes waste.
  • Structured, auditable data. Counts land back in the sheet as clean records instead of free text, ready for reporting.

03 · HOW IT WORKS

04 · RESULTS

<1%of COGS lost to waste, with every lot's usability checked before the next production week
Weeklydata refresh from the data warehouse, with no manual preparation before a count

Waste held under 1% of COGS, fewer stockouts in the following production week because missing stock showed up at the count rather than on the line, and better reporting upwards, built on counts that could be trusted.

05 · WHAT I LEARNED

  • Bring the data to the person doing the work. Showing what should be on the shelf turned counting into checking.
  • Low-code was the right tool: built and changed by operations, without waiting in an engineering queue.
  • A count is only as useful as its structure. Lots and best-before dates as fields, not notes, are what made waste and stockouts visible.
