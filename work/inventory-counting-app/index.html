#005LOW-CODE APPSHIPPED

Inventory Counting App

A low-code app that replaced paper stock counts on the warehouse floor: expected stock by category and lot, quantities ready or discarded, and discrepancies caught live instead of after transcription.

ROLE
Design, build, iterate
YEAR
2025
STACK
Google AppSheet · Google Sheets · data warehouse
STATUS
Shipped, used every week

BUILT · I designed and built it myself in AppSheet, and improved it release by release with the people using it.

01 · PROBLEM

At a global meal-kit company, every production week starts from one question: what stock do we actually have? Ingredients arrive in lots, each with its own best-before date, and what is left from last week decides what can be used next.

Before the app, the count happened on paper sheets in the warehouse. The sheets went to the third-party warehouse's office, where someone transcribed them into a Google Sheet. SKUs got lost and then found, the numbers rarely matched what we expected, and nobody was quite sure which figure to trust.

02 · WHAT I BUILT

  • Expected stock, up front. A navigation bar splits the count into categories, such as expiring stock, rolling stock for the coming week and each ingredient group. Every category lists the SKUs we expect to find, split by lot and so by best-before date.
  • Two numbers per lot. For each lot on the shelf, the worker enters the quantity ready for use and the quantity discarded.
  • Reality wins. An SKU that isn't there can be deleted. Anything untracked can be added with a helper that walks through SKU, supplier (filtered to the ones that actually supply it), lot and best-before date.
  • Shaped by the people using it. Built in 2025, then improved release by release with feedback from its main users: the shop-floor team doing the counts, the third-party warehouse's office staff, production, and food safety and quality assurance (FSQA).
  • Live, then logged. Every entry feeds a Google Sheet as it happens, so discrepancies show up in real time. At the end of the day the app snapshots the inventory and appends every SKU to a master sheet: its final category and where it ended up, whether discarded, donated or rolled into next week.

05 · RESULTS

<1%of COGS lost to waste, with every lot's usability checked before the next production week
Livediscrepancies flagged during the count, instead of days later after transcription

Fewer stockouts in the following production week, because missing stock showed up at the count rather than on the production line. Reporting to leadership got better too: every SKU's outcome, whether discarded, donated or rolled, sat in one master log.

06 · WHAT I LEARNED

  • Remove the hand-offs. Every step between the paper sheet and the spreadsheet was a place where an SKU could get lost.
  • Bring the data to the person doing the work. Showing what should be on the shelf turned counting into checking.
  • Low-code was the right tool: built and changed by operations, without waiting in an engineering queue.
