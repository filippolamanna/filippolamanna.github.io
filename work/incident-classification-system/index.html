#008SHEETS + APISHIPPED

Incident Classification System

A Google Sheet that turned warehouse incident reporting from a ten-field form into a guided, mostly automatic ticket: 90% of the fields filled for you, the title written by rule, and one click to send it.

ROLE
Idea, design, build
YEAR
2024–2026
STACK
SQL · Google Sheets · Apps Script · Freshservice API
STATUS
Shipped

01 · PROBLEM

During inbound and production, the warehouse team flagged every incident to us: a supplier that didn't arrive, SKUs missing from a delivery, quantities that didn't match the order, quality issues, or something missing to complete the boxes.

The chain was long and manual. The shop floor sent a WhatsApp message to the third-party warehouse's office, the office opened a ticket in Freshservice, and urgent ones went on to Slack, where we handled substitutions and supplier escalations. Each ticket meant more than ten fields plus a free-text explanation. Titles depended on who wrote them and how their day was going, and mistakes in the SKU, the quantity or the supplier turned emergencies into chaos.

02 · WHAT I BUILT

  • An API, not another form. I asked the global team for access to the Freshservice API, because the fix was to stop filling the form by hand, not to make the form nicer.
  • The data is already there. A Google Sheet with the same fields as the ticket, loaded daily and automatically from the data warehouse through SQL queries I wrote: incoming deliveries, recipes and the rest.
  • A guided pick. The office chooses the incident type, straight from Freshservice's own list, then the SKU, then the supplier, already filtered to the ones supplying that SKU in the expected deliveries. Everything else fills itself; they only describe the specific issue.
  • Titles written by rule. The title, description and priority are generated from rules for each incident type and sub-type, so every ticket follows the same layout.
  • One button. An Apps Script sends the ticket through the Freshservice API. Nobody needs to open the ticketing interface.

04 · RESULTS

90%of ticket fields filled automatically from warehouse data
1 clickto create the ticket through the API, with no ticketing interface to open

Every ticket now takes one click and follows the same layout, which brought order to emergencies and made the tickets usable for analysis. The critical information, SKU, quantity and supplier, is picked from filtered lists instead of typed, so the mistakes that used to derail an urgent incident mostly disappeared, and priority is set by rule rather than by feel.

05 · WHAT I LEARNED

  • Ask for the API. The biggest improvement came from a permission request, not a new tool.
  • Make the right answer the easy one. Filtered lists removed whole classes of error that training never fixed.
  • Consistent titles are data. Once every ticket reads the same way, you can count, sort and learn from them.
