01 · WHAT IT DOES

Cirulla is a Ligurian card game in the Scopa family: you capture table cards that match your card or add up to 15. There are few good ways to play it on a phone, and card apps often get the regional rules wrong.

This iPhone app plays a full two-player match against the computer at three difficulty levels, following the rules as they are played in Liguria. It keeps the traditional secret declarations (buona and cirulla), shows a full score breakdown after each hand, and saves automatically so you can pick up later.

It is in English and Italian. You choose the cards at first start, from three decks (Ligurian, Classic or the app's own Simple cards), and can switch at any time, even mid-game. Settings also hold the four house rules, capture hints, haptics, match length and a choice of two app icons.

02 · HOW IT WORKS

  • The game engine is a separate Swift package with no user interface. It encodes every rule: captures by equal value, by sum or by sum to 15, the Ace sweep, the seven of hearts (the matta), which takes its value only when you declare with it, the dealer's opening bonus and all end-of-hand scoring including primiera.
  • Every match comes from a seed, so any game can be replayed exactly from its seed and list of moves. That made rule bugs reproducible.
  • Easy plays randomly but prefers captures. Medium looks one move ahead and weighs scopa chances, the seven of diamonds, diamonds, primiera and the risk of leaving a table the opponent can sweep.
  • Hard uses Monte Carlo search: it deals the unseen cards at random many times, plays each option to the end of the hand, and picks the move with the best average result. It only sees what a real player would see.
  • Declarations are never prompted: you triple-tap the table (right half for buona, left for cirulla), the way a real player speaks up. A wrong call gets a small shake and no penalty. A card is played with a tap or by swiping it up.
  • Four Ligurian house rules are on unless you switch them off: cappotto (all ten diamonds wins the match), scciupon de fotta (a scopa with the last card of the hand does not count), a monte (two or more Aces dealt to the table void the deal) and the dealer's four of a kind.
  • Matches run to the traditional 31, 51 or 101 points.
  • Every word on screen and every spoken VoiceOver line is in English and Italian. A test fails if any text in the app is missing its Italian.
  • It can be played with VoiceOver: each card says whose it is, a scopa or a declaration is announced, and buona and cirulla can be declared as VoiceOver actions.

05 · HOW IT WAS BUILT

I made the product and rule decisions: which regional rules and house rules to follow, the three decks, declarations as unprompted gestures, and the "Osteria" look of felt green, cream cards and brass. The code was written with AI assistance (Claude Code): one model wrote the plans and another carried them out, test-first.

Playing it on my phone, I caught a rules error the model had written into the spec: the capture obligation applies to the card you play, not your whole hand. The hard part was rule fidelity. Small misreadings change how the game feels, and only real play exposed them.

06 · WHAT I LEARNED

  • Rules are the product. For a traditional game, being correct matters more than any feature.
  • Keeping the game logic in its own tested package made it possible to change the look and motion without breaking play.
  • Planning in detail first, then building in small tested steps, got to a finished-feeling app fast.
