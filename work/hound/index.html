#001AI AGENTIN PRODUCTION

Hound

A human-supervised LLM agent that holds live support chats with vendors to close cases that have been stuck for weeks, and comes back with a written commitment.

ROLE
Design, build, operate
YEAR
2026
STACK
Python · Playwright · FastAPI · Claude
STATUS
In production

BUILT · I designed the agent, wrote the rules it can't argue its way around and ran it on live cases; the code was written with AI assistance (Claude Code).

01 · PROBLEM

Operations teams build up cases that only move when someone opens a vendor's support chat, waits in a queue, and argues for half an hour until a rep commits to something in writing. It is repetitive and slow, and nobody wants to do it. The cases are well documented. The bottleneck is the conversation.

The counterpart is a real person doing their job, and everything the agent sends is read by them. So the design is about control, not about generation.

02 · WHAT I BUILT

Hound opens the vendor's own chat widget, gets past the triage bot, talks to a human rep turn by turn, and comes back with a written commitment, a reference number, or a clear reason why not. An operator console queues cases, shows live chats, and lets a person pause and take over at any point.

The design bet

Every rule that can be decided without judgement lives in Python with tests, not in the prompt. A prompt can be argued out of a rule by a persuasive rep. A function cannot.

  • Gates decide whether and when to speak: a silence ladder, a hard cap, a minimum gap between replies so the agent never talks over a rep, and a keep-alive so long transfer queues don't time the session out.
  • Output checks decide what may go out: formatting stripped, empty messages refused, a term blocklist enforced.
  • A grounding check reads every proposed message against the case brief and what the rep has said, and blocks claims neither supports. The agent's own earlier messages are left out on purpose, or a claim invented on turn three would certify itself on turn five.
  • Negotiation policy in code: positions a rep can move off get one push-back before the chat may end; hard boundaries end it at once.
  • Human in the loop. A person chooses every case, can pause or take over any chat, and reviews the recap before anything is written back.
  • Race safety: the transcript is re-read right before posting, and a send is dropped if the rep spoke in the meantime.

03 · ARCHITECTURE

One stateless model call per turn, with the case brief as the system prompt and the verbatim transcript as input. A crashed runner loses nothing, and every turn is a logged request and response. The model runs sandboxed with no tools, because its input contains text written by an outside party.

04 · RESULTS

140+cases worked since August 2026, across 230+ live chats with human reps across four vendor channels
2 in 3cases end with a written commitment, an agreed refund, or an escalation with a reference
120+ hof live chat handled by the agent instead of a person, and counting
23 minmedian chat; what is left for a person is queueing cases and reviewing a one-paragraph recap

Every chat Hound handles is one a person no longer has to sit in: at least 20 minutes each, sometimes up to two hours, with two in three ending in a commitment. No baseline was measured for a person doing the same chats; before Hound, these cases sat for weeks waiting for someone to chase them. The time figure counts conversation only. Finding the right support topic and waiting in queues are automated too, and not included. About 1,100 automated tests cover the agent and the console.

05 · WHAT I LEARNED

  • Put the rules where they cannot be negotiated with.
  • Test fakes must return the real system's types and shapes, not tidy ones. Two UI bugs shipped because a fake answered a string where production answered a number.
  • Measure every change on a live case and write down what broke. Most of the design above is a record of specific failures.
